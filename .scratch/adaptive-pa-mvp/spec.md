# Interactive Adaptive Pressure Advance MVP

Status: ready-for-agent

## Problem Statement

I want to calibrate OrcaSlicer's Adaptive Pressure Advance by pointing my phone at printed PA Pattern tests. I open the page and tap Start once; from then on, camera capture and scanning advance automatically while I follow visual movement cues. I do not want to identify candidate lines, read faint labels, judge extrusion defects, choose a PA value, press a shutter, or advance through steps manually. If a view is insufficient, the app shows me how to position the phone and captures the next useful still automatically. After scanning multiple calibration patterns, I want a table I can paste into OrcaSlicer.

The repository has an experimental camera app and a frozen Phase 0 still-image evaluator. The evaluator's measured three-pattern held-out pilot failed: preferred PA, printed flow, printed acceleration, and physical-line selection were each 0/3. That result does not justify treating Luna's visual judgments as reliable calibration. The app must retain a real inconclusive outcome and pass a fresh visual feasibility gate before it can claim autonomous PA calibration works.

## Solution

Build an installable, mobile-first Angular PWA backed by a FastAPI service. The user opens a calibration session, taps Start once, grants camera permission, and points the rear camera at OrcaSlicer 2.3+ herringbone PA Pattern prints. After Start, the app detects pattern regions, automatically captures selected high-quality stills, sends only those stills for analysis, and shows one visual camera cue at a time without further taps. The Luna interaction is a sequence of small steps: identify the pattern, read its printed flow and acceleration, compare nearby corners and select a labelled PA line, then independently assess that choice in a new photo. A useful still advances through identify, metadata, and candidate without more camera movement. A step may use one visual inspection tool round or ask for a better view; verification always starts with a new photograph. The backend tracks which capture produced each answer and accepts a measurement only when the two assessments agree on PA and the V corner's ordinal position for a matched pattern. Multiple calibration patterns remain separate within one persistent session. Confirmed measurements become numeric OrcaSlicer Adaptive PA rows; unresolved patterns remain explicitly inconclusive.

Use the existing Phase 0 evaluator and saved failures as a historical baseline. Investigate its recognition and metadata failures on development observations, then score the iterative app flow on a new, independently annotated held-out cohort. The previously scored three-pattern holdout is consumed. The camera app is an experimental prototype until that visual gate, real-phone behavior, and physical calibration outcomes are validated; a failed resource or model gate cannot be converted into a successful PA result.

## User Stories

1. As a phone user, I want to open the app over HTTPS without installing a native app, so that I can begin calibration on Android or iOS.
2. As a phone user, I want a clear explanation of camera use and selected still-image uploads, so that I know what leaves my device before granting permission.
3. As a phone user, I want the rear camera preview to fill the working screen, so that I can aim at the print while seeing guidance.
4. As a phone user, I want permission denial or camera unavailability explained with a recovery action, so that the session does not silently stall.
5. As a phone user, I want the app to handle phone rotation and preview cropping, so that overlays continue to point at the correct physical region.
6. As a phone user, I want the app to capture suitable stills automatically, so that I do not need to photograph or upload each pattern manually.
7. As a phone user, I want capture to wait for a stable, sharp, adequately exposed view, so that avoidable blur and glare do not consume analysis requests.
8. As a phone user, I want a detail photograph at available camera resolution, so that small labels and extrusion features are not lost to a preview-sized capture.
9. As a phone user, I want unsupported high-resolution capture to fall back to a video frame, so that my camera still works.
10. As a phone user, I want the app to avoid recapturing essentially the same view, so that I am not trapped in a redundant analysis loop.
11. As a phone user, I want an automatically detected pattern region highlighted, so that I can see what print the app is inspecting without selecting it.
12. As a phone user, I want a clear visual cue to move, tilt, hold still, show more of the pattern, or improve lighting, so that I can react without reading instructions.
13. As a phone user, I want one camera adjustment at a time, so that I can follow guidance while watching the preview.
14. As a phone user, I want an overlay that follows the inspection target as the phone moves, so that I can align the requested region.
15. As a phone user, I want the app to correct its movement instruction when the target moves the wrong way, so that guidance reflects the actual camera geometry.
16. As a phone user, I want a new overview requested after tracking is lost, so that stale arrows do not point away from the print.
17. As a phone user, I want the app to try the phone light when supported and useful, so that a dark print can become readable without an extra manual control step.
18. As a phone user, I want the app to assess whether a small angle change reduced glare, so that it does not alternate endlessly between opposing tilt requests.
19. As a phone user, I want the app to capture the requested detail automatically once it is visible and sharp, so that I keep holding the phone without tapping a shutter or Next control.
20. As a phone user, I want progress shown without asking me to choose a candidate line, so that the process stays camera-driven.
21. As a phone user, I want the app to recognize a supported OrcaSlicer herringbone calibration pattern, so that unrelated objects never produce a PA result.
22. As a phone user, I want printed flow and acceleration associated with the correct physical calibration pattern, so that batch results cannot be swapped.
23. As a phone user, I want an unnumbered intervening line left unresolved until its PA can be justified from readable print, so that half-step choices are not guessed.
24. As a phone user, I want corner and transition quality assessed across useful views, so that the chosen PA reflects extrusion evidence rather than label recognition alone.
25. As a phone user, I want unreadable labels to trigger a camera-positioning request, so that missing metadata is not invented.
26. As a phone user, I want a verified overview identity carried to a reliably matched close-up, so that I do not need to show the same printed marks repeatedly.
27. As a phone user, I want contradictory observations to prompt further inspection, so that a single confident-looking model response cannot override the print.
28. As a phone user, I want the same photograph analyzed twice to count as one view, so that repetition cannot manufacture certainty.
29. As a phone user, I want the result marked inconclusive when nearby candidate lines cannot be distinguished, so that I do not paste a fabricated value into OrcaSlicer.
30. As a phone user, I want a completed PA measurement tied to verified flow and acceleration, so that the result is usable for Adaptive PA.
31. As a phone user, I want the app to recognize several calibration patterns on one plate, so that I can complete a batch session without restarting.
32. As a phone user, I want completed patterns remembered when they reappear in view, so that they are not recalibrated accidentally.
33. As a phone user, I want guidance to show the next unmeasured pattern, so that I can progress through a batch by moving the camera.
34. As a phone user, I want to resume an interrupted session with its evidence and outstanding inspection target, so that a page reload does not erase the calibration.
35. As a phone user, I want network and model failures shown as recoverable, so that verified measurements remain available while I retry.
36. As a phone user, I want only completed measurements exported, so that unresolved tests cannot silently enter my slicer settings.
37. As a phone user, I want duplicate flow-and-acceleration pairs flagged before export, so that the table has no conflicting rows.
38. As a phone user, I want a one-tap copy action for numeric PA, flow, acceleration rows, so that I can paste the table directly into OrcaSlicer.
39. As a phone user, I want unresolved patterns listed beside completed results, so that I know what was not measured.
40. As a phone user, I want optional result details, so that I can inspect what the session accepted without making that review mandatory.
41. As a phone user, I want to cancel or delete a session and its stored images, so that I control retained calibration data.
42. As a phone user, I want the camera to stop when I finish or leave the session, so that it no longer records preview frames.
43. As an operator, I want model credentials held only on the backend, so that the browser cannot expose the API key.
44. As an operator, I want bounded processing concurrency and idempotent uploads, so that retries and slow model calls do not duplicate observations or overwhelm the server.
45. As an evaluator, I want real-print results measured against independent annotations and physical verification, so that the app's success claim is based on printing outcomes rather than model confidence.

## Implementation Decisions

- **Scope and dependency.** Target OrcaSlicer 2.3+ herringbone PA Pattern tests, including batch prints with flow and acceleration marks. Preserve the Phase 0 feasibility gate. The saved held-out result failed and cannot be reused as untouched evaluation after prompt or vision changes. Treat a new independent print cohort and physical checks as prerequisites for claiming the calibration MVP complete; without them, the deliverable is an experimental camera prototype with honest inconclusive behavior.
- **Domain model.** A print job can contain many physical calibration patterns. A calibration session contains pattern identities, captured stills, agent phase, and measurements. A still may show multiple pattern regions. Local visual registration links regions across captures; image position alone is insufficient. A completed measurement has PA, flow, acceleration, and status. The backend binds model answers to captures without asking Luna to emit capture IDs.
- **Existing code.** Reuse the Phase 0 evaluator's local pattern localization and cross-view registration where they fit the live workflow. Keep the frozen Phase 0 protocol and results as historical experiment records. The live camera flow uses its own small, iterative request schemas. Do not put the independent answer key or pilot annotations into production model requests.
- **Frontend.** Build an Angular TypeScript PWA with camera, calibration, guidance, results, and core session areas. Start is the only required interaction in the scan flow after camera permission; automatic capture, guidance transitions, polling, and progression require no shutter, upload, or Next taps. Use the phone's rear-facing camera through `getUserMedia`, request a high-resolution still through `ImageCapture.takePhoto` when available, and use a video-frame fallback after a capture error or five-second native capture timeout. Record actual stream and capture dimensions in diagnostics, handle orientation changes and camera interruptions, and stop tracks when the session ends. During scanning, fit the camera preview to the space between a compact header and a bottom guidance bar. Keep the movement pictogram, one status line, and Stop control outside the camera image; show separate capture, upload, queued, and phase-specific analysis states with elapsed waiting time. A stability ring shows actual capture readiness. Put Luna’s marked saved photo and its clean detail crop in a reference strip outside the live image. Label this as a saved photo; it does not track the moving camera. Do not draw an untracked target reticle or show quality metrics over the print. Keep concise text as optional accessibility support.
- **Capture control.** Inspect a downsampled preview locally for motion, sharpness, exposure, target visibility, and redundancy. In the 160×120 preview sample, allow translations of up to three pixels before computing residual motion, so ordinary hand tremor over high-contrast lines does not block capture. Accept two clear samples 450 ms apart; keep sharpness and exposure checks and log both raw and compensated motion. Keep configurable thresholds and a bounded frame buffer. Preserve the original-resolution selected still for detailed analysis; do not upscale low-resolution input and call it new physical detail. Do not stream video to the backend.
- **Guidance.** Luna may request one of four next-view actions: closer, show the full pattern, improve lighting, or hold still. The frontend renders the chosen action as a large visual cue and takes the next sharp, sufficiently different still automatically. A local quality check may temporarily request steadiness, focus, or light. Preserve the requested area and reason when rejecting an unchanged view; do not substitute a generic closer command. Prolonged movement or blur produces a support/focus hint. Show the marked target on its source photo, with an enlarged clean detail, so the user can find the part to show next. After a candidate, use a visual change-angle cue for verification. If registration fails, request a new full-pattern view. Cap unproductive attempts and oscillation. Live spatial overlays still require independently validated tracking.
- **Phone light.** Attempt torch control only when the active camera track reports support. Measure whether exposure or glare improves; provide a lighting-position instruction when torch is unavailable or unhelpful. Never make torch support a prerequisite for calibration.
- **Image path.** Local image processing locates pattern regions and matches them across views before a cropped still reaches the VLM. A failed detector requests a better full-pattern view; a failed match keeps the observations separate. The first image baseline applies orientation and crop only. Any sharpening, contrast change, perspective correction, or new model protocol requires its own validation rather than a silent switch.
- **Backend.** Use FastAPI with SQLite for persisted sessions, observations with processing status, pattern identities, inspection targets, and measurements. An observation upload is acknowledged promptly and processed asynchronously with a concurrency limit. Persist state transitions before returning control. An idempotency key ensures network retries cannot create a second observation. The client must poll the existing observation rather than submit another VLM request while processing is pending. A separate operation table is unnecessary.
- **API contract.** Expose `POST /api/sessions`, `GET /api/sessions/{id}`, `POST /api/sessions/{id}/observations`, `GET /api/sessions/{id}/observations/{observation_id}`, `GET /api/sessions/{id}/guidance`, `GET /api/sessions/{id}/results`, `POST /api/sessions/{id}/export`, `POST /api/sessions/{id}/cancel`, and `DELETE /api/sessions/{id}`. The upload accepts a raw image body with an idempotency header, rejects unsupported formats and oversized images, and returns a session revision, observation ID, and queued status; the observation ID is also the processing-operation identifier polled through the observation read. The read returns processing status, current agent phase/activity with a start time and optional inspection target, and structured results. Authenticated `GET /api/sessions/{id}/observations/{observation_id}/inspection/{inspection_index}/{kind}` serves the exact marked or detail PNG used by the tool; targets bind to immutable images within that observation. Never place session secrets in image URLs. Use stable error codes for invalid image, unsupported pattern, missing metadata, exhausted inspection attempts, and model failure. Generate TypeScript contracts from backend OpenAPI rather than maintaining two hand-written schema copies.
- **Session access.** Return an unguessable session access secret when a session is created and require it for every read or mutation of that session. Keep the secret in local browser storage for recovery; do not treat a guessable session ID as authentication. Offer explicit session deletion. Bound raw-image, temporary-upload, log, and model-response retention.
- **Browser recovery.** Use bounded IndexedDB storage for selected stills and pending uploads, plus the session access secret and revision. On reload, fetch server state, reconcile pending idempotency keys, restore the current agent phase, and resume without discarding verified measurements. Offline mode preserves state and defers upload; it does not pretend to run the VLM locally.
- **VLM boundary.** Keep a provider-neutral adapter boundary and implement GPT-6 Luna first, using stored Responses and backend-only credentials. Send one relevant crop per step after the privacy notice. Use phase-specific questions and small structured replies for identify, metadata, candidate, and verify. Do not send image hashes, capture IDs, source-crop bookkeeping coordinates, or evidence IDs to Luna, and do not ask it to cite them. Offer one `inspect_region` function with a short label and one rectangle in a 0–1000 grid relative to the image Luna sees. Validate the rectangle, render it on a copy with Pillow, and return both the marked image and the unmodified native-resolution detail as actual image tool output. Luna then answers the current small phase question. Allow at most one tool round per phase and count its continuation against the same call/cost budget. The overlay is a pointer, never printed evidence; the original remains intact. The VLM may interpret the print and request one next view; it cannot control the camera, mutate session state, or unilaterally confirm a measurement. Short model reasons explain the selected visual cue. This visual scratchpad is experimental; it does not establish improved detection or PA accuracy.
- **Validation and decision engine.** Validate each small reply for its phase. The candidate and verify steps must both name a readable printed PA and count the best V corner from the lowest printed PA end, including unlabelled corners. The verify step starts without the earlier candidate, marks, or model-response chain; the backend compares the two PA values and ordinal positions from distinct captures matched to one pattern. Exact duplicate image content reuses its first processing result. Unreadable labels, conflicting line choices, resource exhaustion, or missing printed flow or acceleration produce an inconclusive measurement. Bound captures and provider spend. Local digests may remain for upload deduplication and secret protection; they do not enter the model conversation, response log, or user-facing result rows.
- **State machine.** Persist the identify, metadata, candidate, and verify agent phase per pattern, plus awaiting-detail, complete, unsupported, or inconclusive status. Network interruption and provider failure are recoverable states, not session resets. Every session transition carries a revision so stale client responses cannot replace newer guidance.
- **Batch behavior.** Identify each physical calibration pattern by verified printed metadata and geometry in session context. Keep evidence separate for patterns on one plate. After a measurement completes, seek another visible unmeasured pattern automatically. When no new pattern is found after bounded guidance attempts, end scanning and show results without requiring a Finish tap. Reappearance of a completed pattern must not silently recalibrate it.
- **Export.** Include only confirmed, internally consistent measurements with unique flow-and-acceleration pairs. If duplicate pairs disagree, block those rows and show the conflict; if they are exact duplicates, include one row. Emit one numeric `PA,FLOW,ACCELERATION` triplet per line, decimal point regardless of locale, no header, and precision matching verified marks. The results screen offers one copy button, lists excluded unresolved patterns, and provides optional result details.
- **Security and privacy.** Obtain camera permission through the browser, explain selected still-image upload to the configured AI provider, crop to the print when possible, and strip incidental metadata. Authenticate session access and support deletion. Preserve only bounded selected stills, not a continuous video stream. The user has accepted provider-side stored Responses for the experiment; retention for the app must be documented clearly rather than described as zero retention.
- **Delivery order.** Keep the experimental camera slice available for the user's interaction trial. Use its logs and saved stills to resolve visual failures, then score a fresh held-out cohort and verify physical prints before claiming a reliable autonomous calibration MVP.

## Testing Decisions

- **Primary new seam.** Drive a complete session through the phone UI and public API using recorded camera frames and recorded VLM responses at those external boundaries. Assert that one Start tap leads to visual guidance, automatic overview and detail capture, automatic progression, persisted observations, and a result or inconclusive status without shutter or Next taps. Test user-observable behavior and stable API results, not internal helper calls or duplicated implementation logic.
- **Existing seam and prior art.** Keep the Phase 0 evaluator command and its recorded-provider tests as a frozen baseline. Its saved development and held-out reports show failure, not a passing benchmark. Exercise the active camera API with recorded Luna replies to check phase progression and duplicate upload behavior; real phone interaction still needs the user's trial.
- **Visual gate.** Measure detector localization, cross-view matching, supported-pattern recognition, printed-label and metadata extraction, correct physical candidate-line association, exact preferred PA, one-increment agreement, inconclusive behavior, and inspection-target utility on a new independently annotated held-out cohort. Freeze quantitative acceptance thresholds before seeing that cohort and report exact and one-increment agreement separately. Keep all photos of one physical pattern in one split. Do not tune against or rescore the consumed three-pattern holdout as fresh evidence. Missing acceptable-range annotations remain unscorable.
- **Camera gate.** On real Android and iOS phones over HTTPS, verify permission, rear-camera selection, still resolution, preview/overlay alignment in both orientations, automatic sharp capture, interruption recovery, and supported or unsupported torch behavior. Use recorded or simulated frame sequences to test blur, glare, target too small or off-screen, wrong-direction movement, focus limits, rotation, and lost tracking. Confirm guidance improves the view and terminates rather than oscillating indefinitely.
- **Agent and API gate.** Through the public API, check phase progression, unreadable metadata, disagreement between candidate and verify, repeated image content, malformed model responses, invalid annotation coordinates, image endpoint authentication, cancellation during inspection, duplicate uploads, session deletion, and duplicate flow/acceleration pairs. Verify that the marked and detail image bytes served to the UI are exactly those sent back to the model and that progress is visible before the model finishes. Confirm no failed path fabricates or exports a measurement. Inspect the live Luna request text and schema to ensure they contain no image hash or evidence ID fields.
- **Batch and export gate.** Test multiple patterns in one photograph and across changing camera positions. Confirm the result for each physical pattern uses its own printed flow, acceleration, and line selection. Export only confirmed unique rows in numeric headerless OrcaSlicer format, with copy behavior verified in the browser.
- **Physical gate.** Compare accepted values with independent human inspection of real prints. For a representative subset, print verification objects at selected PA values and inspect corner and extrusion quality. Record whether disagreements arise from localization, labels, metadata, visual assessment, camera quality, or the decision rule. Model confidence alone cannot satisfy this gate.
- **Completion evidence.** Automated suites must pass, real-phone behavior must be documented, and the visual and physical results must support a reliable completed measurement or an explicit inconclusive outcome. If additional independent prints or ratings are unavailable, report the missing validation and label the build an experimental prototype, not a completed calibration MVP.

## Out of Scope

- OrcaSlicer versions before 2.3, tower tests, arbitrary third-party generators, or other calibration types.
- Direct Klipper, Moonraker, printer, or toolhead-camera control; automatic pattern printing; automatic filament-profile edits.
- Full-plate calibration without moving the phone, training a dedicated extrusion neural network, and a general-purpose image annotation tool.
- Mandatory manual line selection, manual PA correction, or user assessment of extrusion in the normal app workflow.
- Claims of accuracy across printers, materials, lighting, or print jobs based only on the existing single-job photo set.
